The molecule

NMN (β-Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) is a naturally occurring molecule your body uses as the direct precursor to NAD⁺ — a coenzyme essential for cellular energy, repair and signalling. Supplementing NMN is a researched way to support NAD⁺ levels that decline with age.

Both are NAD⁺ precursors. NR (nicotinamide riboside) is one step further upstream and is converted into NMN, which then becomes NAD⁺. NMN is the more direct precursor and the form with growing human-trial evidence. We use stabilized β-NMN.

Human trials to date report NMN is generally well-tolerated at studied doses. That said, supplements affect people differently — consult your physician before starting, especially if pregnant, nursing, or on medication.†
